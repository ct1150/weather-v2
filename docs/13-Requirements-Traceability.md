## Classification and decision rules

- **Hard** means the source states a product, engineering, governance, or operating obligation. It maps only to an Active Hard Requirement.
- **Suggestion** means optional ideation, a benefit hypothesis, an alternative, or unapproved later scope. **Example** means illustrative names, values, prices, scores, counts, routes, brands, or delivery form. Because the current Active authority set defines no Guidance requirements, both classifications use `NONE` with a rationale-only decision.
- **Covered** preserves source intent. **Changed** preserves the approved intent with an explicit controlled correction. **Rejected** records an approved exclusion or alternative. Every Changed or Rejected rationale starts with the exact Product Owner approval record.
- Top 100, sample prices and scores, named example-city output, a 150–250-page target, rejected alternative branding, and autonomous future AI writing are not Hard obligations. Prices and factual AI-assisted content become Hard only through their authorized-data and human-review contracts.
- The matrix contains no unresolved decision: the machine-record `Needs Decision` count is zero.
- Structural validation proves schema, links, hashes, and a complete partition; it does **not** prove semantic ownership. Semantic evidence comes from the sentence-level audit summarized in the Task 7 report and embodied identically in both representations below.
